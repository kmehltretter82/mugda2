3. The three abstractions that would make this work
A. Algorithms whose state includes their memory
For Cunningham, the state must contain at least
\[
s=(\text{basis or policy},\ \text{cyclic cursor}).
\]
For broader rules, add their additional memory.

Your existing development already includes a cursor in the simplex state and a last component in the MDP state. That is exactly the right starting point. Family definitions

Mugda2 should make these dependencies visible in the type of an algorithm. A transformation that preserves policies but loses the cursor has not preserved Cunningham’s execution.

I would also distinguish several costs:
Cost	What it measures
Pivots or policy switches	The quantity in the current lower-bound theorem
Candidate inspections	Work performed while scanning for the next improving action
Arithmetic operations	Exact additions, multiplications, comparisons, and linear algebra
Bit operations	The cost of manipulating growing rational numerators and denominators


These distinctions matter mathematically. A pivot bound is one component of a runtime bound.
B. Transformations that preserve executions and costs
The important interface is a simulation theorem. Schematically,
\[
R(s,t)\ \land\ s\xrightarrow{c}_A s'
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\exists t'.\ t\xRightarrow{c'}_B t'\ \land\ R(s',t').
\]
Here \(R\) relates states in two representations, and \(c,c'\) describe costs.

For a one-to-one pivot correspondence, require \(c'=c\). For a more general simulation, prove the inequality needed for the intended upper or lower bound.

The contract must cover:
- Initial states.
- Improving-action tests.
- Ordering and history updates.
- Leaving-variable choices and relevant ties.
- Terminal states.
- Cost accounting.
This would become the common interface for game-to-MDP, MDP-to-LP, rational-to-real, and gadget transformations.

This is also a potentially interesting programming-language contribution: a usable system for expressing and composing algorithm transformations while preserving quantitative behavior.
C. Symbolic certificates for very long executions
A theorem about \(2^n\) pivots should not require constructing and checking \(2^n\) individual steps.

Instead, expose the structure of the lower-bound proof:
- A gadget invariant.
- A phase-entry condition.
- A phase-exit condition.
- A relation between counter states.
- A recurrence for the number of pivots.
For example, a certificate might establish
\[
T(n+1)\geq 2T(n)+d,
\]
with proved base cases and a theorem connecting these phases to legal algorithm executions.

Mugda2 could offer compositional syntax for these certificates. Lean would verify the composition lemmas and induction.

This creates a useful contrast within the application itself: a small generator produces a small instance; that instance induces a very long algorithm execution; a structured proof explains that execution without enumerating it.

Keep the encoding theorem explicit. If
\[
\operatorname{pivots}(G(n))\geq 2^{cn},
\qquad
\operatorname{bits}(G(n))\leq Cn^d,
\]
the resulting lower bound in terms of encoding length is of the form
\[
2^{\Omega(\operatorname{bits}(G(n))^{1/d})}.
\]
It does not automatically become exponential in the total number of input bits.
4. How Hofmann, Abel, and Schwichtenberg fit this application
Their ideas can contribute to different parts of one coherent design.
Abel: make recursive constructions and proof procedures manageable
The sized-types component is useful for:
- Recursively assembling gadget families.
- Traversing certificates.
- Constructing bounded execution prefixes.
- Defining recursive transformations.
- Expressing structural invariants across higher-order helper functions.
It should establish that these computations are well-founded.

However, termination and efficiency remain separate obligations. An \(n\)-bit countdown has a compact ranking function and can still take \(2^n-1\) steps. The rank’s description is small; its numerical range is large.

That distinction could become a defining strength of Mugda2: users see separately why a program terminates and what bounds its resource consumption.
Hofmann: resource annotations and potential functions
Hofmann’s work with collaborators on automatic amortized analysis provides a concrete model: types carry quantitative information, constraints are generated, and solving those constraints yields resource bounds. Resource Aware ML demonstrates this approach for polynomial resource bounds on first-order functional programs. cs.cmu.edu

For Mugda2, a resource contract might require
\[
\operatorname{cost}(s,s')+\Phi(s')\leq\Phi(s).
\]
The system could search for \(\Phi\), then produce evidence that the inequality holds.

In the pivot-rule application, related machinery could help find and verify:
- Transience certificates.
- Bounds for instance generators.
- Bounds for certificate checkers.
- Algebraic inequalities controlling gadget behavior.
- Perturbation margins.
The exponential lower-bound proof would still need its own logical structure. Standard upper-bound amortized analysis does not automatically derive it.
Schwichtenberg: turn constructive mathematics into useful programs
The natural computational outputs here are:
- Explicit hard-instance generators.
- Exact policy evaluators.
- Transformations between models.
- Certificate checkers.
- Procedures that explain a failed invariant.
Schwichtenberg’s work on extracting programs and identifying computationally irrelevant proof components is directly relevant to making those outputs practical. mathematik.uni-muenchen.de

For this project, maintain an explicit executable construction alongside its correctness theorem. An existential theorem proved with noncomputable machinery does not by itself provide the executable tool you want in the browser.
Cubical and HoTT ideas
I would keep these as a later foundational research track.

The hypercubes occurring in unique-sink orientations are finite combinatorial objects; their presence does not require cubical type theory. Moreover, an equivalence between configuration types does not establish preservation of the transition rule, cursor, or cost.

There may eventually be interesting work on transporting structured algorithms and their proofs across equivalences. For the first scientific application, explicit simulations in the existing Lean setting are sufficient and give a much shorter route to results.
5. The mathematical research program I would prioritize
I would organize this into a foundation, a first quantitative theorem, and two deeper research directions.
A. Complete the reusable execution and geometry layer
These are valuable formalization targets with fairly clear specifications.

First, certify an executable evaluator. For example, an external or Mugda2 implementation can propose a policy-value vector \(v\). A checker verifies
\[
(I-P_\pi)v=r_\pi.
\]
Under a proved uniqueness condition, this establishes agreement with the abstract policy value. Similar certificates can support basis solutions, reduced costs, and pivot decisions.

This lets you use efficient computation while keeping a simple mathematical checking interface.

Second, formalize rational-to-real transport. The mission explicitly records that its geometry is over rational points and that the real-scalar justification has not yet been turned into a checked transport theorem. Mission scope

There are two related tasks:
1. Preserve the rationally computed trajectory after embedding coefficients into \(\mathbb R\): determinants, feasibility, reduced-cost signs, ratio comparisons, and cursor updates.
2. Establish the corresponding real polyhedral statements about vertices, faces, boundedness, and adjacency.
Third, formalize the direct game-to-simplex connection. Matthew Maat’s recent work gives a direct correspondence between strategy improvement and simplex, under appropriate nondegeneracy assumptions, and connects the geometry with lopsided sets. This is a natural next major known theorem to formalize using the common simulation interface. arxiv.org

These results would make future lower-bound proofs easier to transfer and compare.
B. First quantitative target: the effective-horizon bound
The candidate from your earlier plan is particularly attractive because there is a concrete proof route.

Consider a finite maximizing MDP. Let \(P(s,a,t)\) describe transitions among nonterminal states, and let \(m\) be the total number of state-action pairs. Assume exact policy evaluation and strictly improving single-action switches.

Suppose there is a certificate \(w\) and a number \(H\geq1\) such that
\[
1\leq w_s\leq H,
\qquad
\sum_t P(s,a,t)w_t\leq w_s-1
\]
for every state-action pair.

The target is
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{CunninghamPivots}
=O\!\left(m^2H\log(2H)\right).
}
\]
Status: I have a plausible proof argument for this precisely specified cyclic algorithm. It is not yet Lean-checked, and I have not established that the statement is new.

The transience certificate itself is established theory: Feinberg–Huang give this kind of characterization and derive complexity bounds for Howard and Dantzig variants. Scherrer develops the nearby contraction-and-action-elimination machinery. The cyclic scheduling argument is the part to isolate and investigate. people.orie.cornell.edu

Here is the proof strategy.

1. Define a sweep correctly.

A sweep means \(m\) consecutive action inspections in cyclic order. It does not mean \(m\) accepted pivots.

Each improving action triggers a switch and exact reevaluation; other actions are skipped.

2. Show that a sweep gives Bellman progress.

If \(v\) is the value vector at the start and \(v'\) at the end, prove
\[
v'\geq \mathcal T v,
\]
where \(\mathcal T\) is the Bellman optimality operator.

Every action is inspected. At its inspection, either its backup is already below the current value or switching establishes the necessary improvement. Monotonicity preserves that progress through the rest of the sweep.

3. Obtain weighted contraction.

Using
\[
\|x\|_w=\max_s\frac{|x_s|}{w_s},
\]
the certificate yields contraction factor
\[
q=1-\frac1H.
\]
Thus each full sweep contracts the optimality error.

4. Turn convergence into finite action elimination.

This is the essential step; contraction alone is insufficient.

At the start of a phase, let
\[
e=v^*-v^\pi,
\qquad
\eta=\|e\|_w>0.
\]
Choose a state attaining the maximum weighted error. Its current action has optimal-value disadvantage at least \(\eta\). Consequently, any policy retaining that action has weighted error at least \(\eta/H\).

After \(O(H\log(2H))\) sweeps, contraction pushes the error below that threshold. That action cannot reappear, because policy values continue improving.

There are at most \(m\) actions to eliminate, and at most \(m\) switches per sweep. This yields the proposed bound.

This is an excellent first Mugda2 benchmark: the drift certificate, sweep semantics, contraction proof, elimination argument, and cost accounting are distinct reusable components.

A further generalization would replace cyclic order by a schedule with a bounded inspection cover time \(B\), aiming at
\[
O\!\left(mBH\log(2H)\right).
\]
Then your lower-bound formalization becomes useful on the other side. For a family with \(m=O(n)\), combining a \(2^n\) lower bound with this upper bound would force
\[
H\log(2H)\geq \frac{2^n}{\operatorname{poly}(n)}.
\]
That would precisely connect long executions to the family’s quantitative transience. It would also explain why a bound polynomial in \(H\) can coexist with an exponential lower bound in the construction size.
C. Larger target: compact realizations of broader hard families
This is where the recent literature sharpens the ambition.

Disser, Loho, Maat, and Mosis’s STACS 2026 paper already proves lower bounds for broad index-based and ranking-based rule classes. For the index-based result, the LP translation uses doubly exponential coefficients; the authors explicitly identify this as a limitation when interpreting the result in terms of binary input length. drops.dagstuhl.de

Maat’s direct correspondence also discusses this coefficient-growth problem. arxiv.org

A substantive target is therefore:
For a specified family of adversarial games and a specified rule class, construct polynomial-dimension, polynomial-bit rational LPs that preserve the observations and execution needed for the lower bound.

Mugda2 could help by making each preservation requirement explicit:
- Which comparisons determine a rule’s behavior?
- Which numerical inequalities force those comparisons?
- Which coefficient hierarchies are necessary?
- Can a large parameter be replaced by a smaller rational witness?
- Can one prove the replacement uniformly for all family sizes?
Agents could search for simplified coefficients on small instances, derive candidate symbolic inequalities, and propose a general construction. Lean would check the universal argument.

Focus this project on the new generalized hard families. Large numerical values in the original Cunningham construction do not by themselves imply large binary encodings.

A compact reduction for arbitrary parity or mean-payoff games would have much larger complexity consequences. A carefully chosen family is a more credible initial research target.
D. Larger target: quantitative robustness of Cunningham lower bounds
A second strong question is:
How much can the hard instance change while the long execution remains unavoidable under the specified rule and start?

The desired theorem could look like
\[
\|\Delta I\|\leq\varepsilon(n)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{pivots}(I_n+\Delta I)\geq 2^{cn},
\]
with a precise normalization and perturbation model.

The work lies in controlling all decisions along the execution:
- Signs of actions scanned before the selected action.
- Positivity of the selected improvement.
- Leaving-variable comparisons.
- Feasibility and nonsingularity.
- Ties and degeneracy.
- The initial basis and cursor.
A computable, explicitly bounded small radius would be a good intermediate result. An inverse-polynomial robustness radius in a meaningful normalized model would be substantially more ambitious.

There is already strong nearby work: Christ–Yannakakis prove robust lower bounds for Howard’s policy iteration. A Cunningham-specific result needs to be positioned against that literature, with careful attention to which perturbations are permitted. arxiv.org

For Mugda2, this is a rich application of proof-producing inequality solvers and parameterized certificates.
E. Later frontier: memory and information available to the rule
The 2026 ranking-based paper also asks whether its lower bound extends beyond the established small-memory regime. This gives a concrete frontier rather than the vague goal “handle more pivot rules.” Its measure is memory states, which must be distinguished from memory bits. drops.dagstuhl.de

Mugda2 could define a language of controllers whose permitted observations are explicit:
- Global index rankings.
- Reduced-cost rankings.
- Objective-improvement rankings.
- A bounded internal state.
- Particular access to history.
Then one can ask exactly which information or memory allows a controller to escape a construction.

I would leave randomization and genuine network-flow LPs for later. Each requires additional mathematical machinery: an unlucky random trajectory does not establish an expected lower bound, and an MDP-derived LP does not automatically have a network incidence matrix.
6. What the web UI and LLM agents should actually do
The most useful interface would let the researcher move between an example, an explanation, and a theorem.

For a selected family size, it could show:
- The game or MDP graph.
- The current policy or LP basis.
- The cursor and candidates scanned.
- Exact reduced costs or action advantages.
- The counter phase and relevant invariant.
- The corresponding Lean statement and proof status.
For large executions, the user should navigate certified phases rather than wait for every pivot. Selecting a phase would reveal its entry conditions, exit conditions, and proved step count.

A failed experiment should be mathematically informative:
“The proposed simplification fails at this policy: an earlier action in the cyclic order becomes improving.”

That gives an agent a concrete repair task.

The agent workflow should follow the structure of the research:
1. Generate candidates: smaller coefficients, modified gadgets, potential functions, perturbation margins.
2. Run exact experiments: search for counterexamples and minimize failures.
3. Extract a symbolic hypothesis: identify the invariant that explains the examples.
4. Prove the hypothesis: produce parameterized lemmas and certificates.
5. Check and reuse: expose the accepted result as a library theorem.
The universal proof is the decisive step. Success on many small examples remains experimental evidence.

The useful Lean lessons here are practical: stable libraries, searchable theorem names, fast feedback, structured proof states, reproducible builds, and examples that contributors can modify. Agents should receive exact goals and local contexts, not merely screenshots or prose descriptions.

For Prove2Me, the work naturally decomposes into linked missions such as:
- Correctness of the executable policy evaluator.
- Preservation of cursor-aware executions.
- Rational-to-real run transport.
- Soundness of a symbolic phase certificate.
- The fair-sweep lemma.
- Action elimination and the horizon bound.
Each mission should have a fixed statement and explicit dependencies, so progress composes.

I would run one primary open mathematical problem at a time, while agents work on independent supporting lemmas and experiments.

The project could produce three distinct contributions:
1. A formalization contribution: reusable, checked mathematics spanning games, policy iteration, simplex, and geometry.
2. A programming-language contribution: a practical way to combine sized constructions, quantitative contracts, executable witnesses, and compositional certificates.

8. The first milestone I would choose
I would start with this:
Given a parameterized hard family, Mugda2 generates an exact instance, explains its cursor-aware execution, and produces a Lean-checked symbolic certificate for its lower bound. The same framework accepts a transience certificate and supports the proof of a quantitative upper bound.
